How to Create the Perfect Custom Spin Wheel for Your Next Giveaway

Picking a giveaway winner in front of a live audience is one of the most satisfying uses of a spin wheel — everyone sees the exact same random moment you do. Here's how to set one up properly, from loading entries to announcing the winner without a single fairness complaint.

🎡 Open the Free Wheel Generator

1Why a Spin Wheel Beats a Random Number Generator

A plain random number generator technically does the same job as a wheel, but it misses the point of a public giveaway: people want to watch the moment of selection. A spinning wheel slowing down toward a winner creates suspense that a printed number never will. It also gives you a natural screen-recording moment for social proof — viewers can see the spin happen live, which builds trust that nothing was rigged.

For creators and small businesses, that visible fairness matters as much as the prize itself. A properly run raffle depends on people believing the process was honest, and a wheel is the easiest way to prove it.

2Setting Up Your Entry List

Start by collecting your entries in one place — a spreadsheet, comment export, or sign-up form list works fine. Then bring them into your wheel using bulk import instead of typing each name individually:

  • Paste one entry per line into the bulk-add box
  • Double-check for duplicate names or typos before spinning
  • Decide whether every entrant gets one slice, or whether some should get extra chances (see weighting below)
  • Keep a backup copy of your entry list outside the wheel in case you need to re-verify a winner later

If you're running recurring giveaways, save your list as a template so next time only takes a minute to update.

3Weighting Entries Fairly

Sometimes not every entrant should have an equal shot — someone who shared your post twice, referred a friend, or has been a subscriber longer might earn extra chances. The cleanest way to weight a wheel without making it look rigged is to simply add that person's name to the wheel multiple times (once per extra entry), rather than resizing their slice. This keeps every visible slice the same size, so the wheel still looks fair even though some names appear more than once.

4Removing Winners After Each Spin

If you're giving away more than one prize, turn on "remove entry after spin" so a winner can't be picked twice. This single setting is the difference between a giveaway that feels professional and one that generates confused comments asking why the same name came up again. After each spin, briefly show the updated (shorter) entry list before spinning again, so viewers can see the winner was actually removed.

5Matching Your Wheel to Your Brand

A quick theme swap makes your wheel feel like part of your event instead of a generic tool. Pick colors that match your logo or stream overlay, and keep the same theme across every giveaway you run so your audience recognizes it instantly. For more on this, see our full guide to custom wheel themes and visuals.

6Sharing Your Wheel Link

Once your wheel is set up, copy its unique share link and post it alongside your giveaway announcement. This lets entrants see the exact same entry list and wheel you'll use to pick a winner — no need to trust a screenshot after the fact. It also means if you need to re-run the giveaway (say, a winner doesn't respond), you or a co-host can pull up the same wheel from any device.

7Quick Pre-Spin Checklist

  • Entry list is final and free of duplicates or typos
  • Weighted entries (if any) are added as repeated names, not resized slices
  • "Remove entry after spin" is on if giving away multiple prizes
  • Theme matches your brand or stream
  • Wheel link is ready to share publicly
  • Screen recording or stream is running before you hit spin
