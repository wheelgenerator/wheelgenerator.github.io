A raffle only works if people trust the result. Whether you're running a fundraiser for a school, a community event, or a giveaway for your streaming audience, here's how to keep an online wheel provably fair from first spin to last.

1Why Fairness Perception Matters as Much as Actual Fairness

Even a perfectly random wheel can generate complaints if the process feels opaque. People don't just want a fair result — they want to be able to see that it was fair. That means visible entry lists, a public spin (live or recorded), and a process that doesn't change between rounds.

2Confirming Genuine Randomness

A well-built wheel uses physics-based random velocity for each spin rather than a predictable pattern, so results are independent of previous spins. If you're publicly promoting a raffle, it's worth mentioning this in your rules so entrants understand the mechanism isn't just decorative — the visual spin corresponds to a real random outcome, not a pre-decided one.

3Removing Entries as You Go

For multi-prize raffles, always remove a winner immediately after they're announced. This is the single most common integrity issue in DIY raffles — forgetting to remove a name and having them "win" a second prize, which understandably upsets everyone else entered. Show the updated, shorter list before each subsequent spin.

4Why Spin History Matters

Keeping a visible log of every spin and its result protects you if someone questions the outcome later. If a winner doesn't respond and you need to re-draw, history tracking shows exactly what happened and when, rather than relying on memory or a scrolled-past chat log.

5Tips for Streamers & Content Creators

  • Pin the entry list or wheel link in chat before spinning so viewers can follow along
  • Narrate what you're doing as you set up entries — viewers who join mid-stream should be able to catch up quickly
  • Clip or screenshot each winning spin for your own records and for any follow-up questions
  • Match your wheel's theme to your channel branding — see our themes guide — so it feels native to your stream, not like an external tool

6Handling Disputes Gracefully

Even with a fair process, someone will occasionally question a result. Having a public wheel link, a visible entry list, and a recorded spin gives you concrete evidence to point to, which resolves most disputes in seconds rather than turning into a drawn-out argument.
